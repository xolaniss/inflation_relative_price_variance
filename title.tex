\title{Inflation Dynamics and Relative Price Dispersion in South Africa: Implications for Optimal Inflation Targeting}
\author {Keagile Lesame\footnote{Corresponding author. National Treasury, South Africa. Email: Keagile.Lesame@treasury.gov.za} \and
Xolani Sibande\footnote{Economic Research Department, South African Reserve Bank. Email: xolani.sibande@resbank.co.za.}}


\date{\today}
\maketitle


\begin{abstract}

\sethlcolor{red!20}\hl{This paper investigates the dynamic relationship between headline inflation and relative price dispersion (RPD) in the context of inflation target changes in South Africa over 2009 - 2025. We use semi-parametric methods and test the predictions of the menu cost and signal extraction models and extend the analysis by employing quantile-on-quantile regression to capture state-dependent effects across the distribution of relative price variability. Our evidence shows that the relationship is nonlinear and regime-dependent. Specifically, RPD is lowest at headline inflation of about 5 per cent before 2017 and about 3.5 per cent after 2017 and rises with inflation above these levels. Unexpected inflation raises RPD roughly symmetrically before 2017, consistent with signal extraction models, but has no detectable effect after 2017. These results are robust to bootstrap confidence bands, exchange rate and oil price controls, and a core measure of RPD that excludes food and energy. The results suggest that a 3 per cent target is not costly, although this is only one of several considerations relevant to the choice of an inflation target. The findings have important implications for monetary policy design in emerging economies aiming to lower inflation targets while minimizing distortive effects on price signals and resource allocation.}

\end{abstract}
